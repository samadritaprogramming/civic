Three Branches of Government

The U.S. government is divided into three branches. Learn what each branch does and how they keep one another in check.

⏱ 5 minutes 📚 Government Foundations
Lesson progress 72%
01

Why are there three branches?

The U.S. government is divided into three branches: the legislative, executive, and judicial branches.

This structure helps prevent too much power from being concentrated in one place. Each branch has its own responsibilities, while also having ways to limit the power of the other branches.

💡
Think of it this way

Three branches means three different jobs — making laws, enforcing laws, and interpreting laws.

02

The Legislative Branch

The legislative branch is Congress. Congress is made up of two chambers: the House of Representatives and the Senate.

Congress's main job is to create and pass federal laws. Members of Congress are elected to represent people from their states and districts.

🏛️

LEGISLATIVE

Congress

Makes federal laws

03

The Executive Branch

The executive branch is led by the president. Its main responsibility is to enforce and carry out federal laws.

The executive branch includes the president, vice president, Cabinet departments, and many federal agencies.

🇺🇸

EXECUTIVE

President & Federal Agencies

Enforces federal laws

04

The Judicial Branch

The judicial branch interprets laws and determines how they apply to particular cases.

The federal judiciary includes the Supreme Court and other federal courts.

⚖️

JUDICIAL

Federal Courts

Interprets federal laws
